THE IDEA BEHIND IT

Why do we only
check until √n?

n = a × b

If n is composite, it has a pair of factors a and b. If both were greater than √n, then a × b > n — impossible.

At least one factor must be ≤ √n. For n ≥ 2, if no integer from 2 to √n divides n, the number is prime.

THE IDEA BEHIND IT

How the Euclidean Algorithm Works

a = bq + r

a = dividend · b = divisor
q = quotient · r = remainder

  1. Take two positive integers.
  2. Put the larger number first.
  3. Divide it by the smaller number.
  4. Find the quotient and remainder.
  5. Replace the larger number by the smaller.
  6. Replace the smaller by the remainder.
  7. Repeat the division.
  8. Stop when the remainder is zero.
  9. The last non-zero remainder is the GCD (or the final divisor if the first remainder is zero).
