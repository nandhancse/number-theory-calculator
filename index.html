Why do we check only up to √n?
n = a × b

If both factors a and b were greater than √n, their product would be greater than n, which is impossible. Therefore a composite number has at least one factor ≤ √n.

If no integer up to √n divides n, then n is prime.

VIVA NOTES

The mathematics behind RSA

Open any card for a concise explanation you can present.

Prime Numbers

A prime number is greater than 1 and has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself. Distinct primes p and q make φ(pq) easy to calculate.

√n Prime Testing

A composite number has a factor no greater than its square root. Testing 2 through ⌊√n⌋ is therefore enough to prove primality.

GCD & Euclidean Algorithm

The GCD is the greatest positive divisor shared by two integers. Repeatedly apply a = bq + r until r = 0; the last non-zero remainder is the GCD.

Coprime Numbers

Two numbers are coprime when GCD(a, b) = 1. RSA requires e and φ(n) to be coprime so e has a modular inverse.

Modular Arithmetic

a mod n is the remainder after dividing a by n. Like a clock, values wrap around after reaching n. RSA uses this wrapping to keep powers within a fixed range.

Euler’s Totient

φ(n) counts positive integers below n that are coprime to n. For distinct primes p and q, φ(pq) = (p − 1)(q − 1).

Public & Private Keys

The public key (e, n) encrypts. The private key (d, n) decrypts. This lab shows both so the mathematics can be studied.

Encryption & Decryption

Encode a character as M. Encrypt C = Me mod n. Decrypt M = Cd mod n. Because ed ≡ 1 mod φ(n), the original value is recovered.
